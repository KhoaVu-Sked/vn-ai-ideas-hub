Section 01

What an admin can do

Four extra tools, and authority over every idea rather than only the ones you lead.

An administrator account is a workspace-level role, separate from the per-idea roles everyone holds. It is set on your account by another administrator; there is no way to grant it to yourself.

CapabilityMemberProject LeadAdmin
Submit, like, follow, commentYesYesYes
Join a team in any roleYesYesYes
Edit an idea's content—Own ideaAny idea
Change an idea's status—Own ideaAny idea
Triage requests—Own ideaAny idea
Edit team roles, transfer the lead——Yes
Remove someone from a team——Yes
Delete an idea—Request onlyYes
Dashboard, Tasks, Activity, Manage——Yes
Worth knowing

A Project Lead cannot manage their own team. Adding a role, removing a member or transferring the lead all require an administrator — so those requests will come to you.

Section 02

The admin top bar

Four items appear that members never see.

The Manage menu opens on hover, so you can jump straight to a section without loading the page first. Clicking Manage itself opens the page with a dropdown to switch sections.

Section 03

Dashboard

The leadership view. Read the flags first — they are the only part that asks you to act.

A period switch at the top offers All time or This quarter, which filters the idea-based figures.

What each tile means

TileHow it is calculated
Total ideasEvery idea in the period, with the count added this quarter beneath.
Active (in review → pilot)Ideas at In Review, Approved, In Progress or Pilot. Work in flight.
LaunchedIdeas at Launched, and what share of all ideas that is.
Team participationThe share of accounts that have taken part in at least one idea.
Est. hours saved / weekNot yet tracked. The tile is a placeholder until the team agrees how to measure it.

Needs attention

Two rules raise a flag, both about ideas going quiet rather than ideas being wrong:

  • An idea has been In Review for more than 7 days — the team's review target.
  • An idea is On Hold with no update from its lead for an extended period.

Below these sit Ideas by category & engagement, an Ideas by engagement table (likes, requests, members and target per idea) and Top contributors.

Read with care

Top contributors currently scores ideas × 5 + requests × 1 + team memberships × 2. That rewards volume rather than outcome — someone who comments frequently can outrank someone who led an idea to launch. A replacement is drafted in docs/contribution-scoring.md. Treat the current ranking as indicative, not as a performance measure.

Section 04

Manage — Tags

The category list offered on the submit form.

Add a tag, remove one, and set each one's colour. The colour is used for the chip on every card and idea page, so pick distinguishable ones.

Safe to change

Removing a tag strips it from every idea that carries it. No idea is deleted. Renaming is not supported — add the new tag, retag, then remove the old one.

Outstanding decision

The list is still Work / Personal Development / Family / Home, carried over from the original prototype. The proposed categories are Support/CX, Internal Ops, Knowledge, Reporting and Other. Changing it takes a couple of minutes here once the team agrees.

Section 05

Manage — Form fields

Extra questions on the submit form, beyond the six built-in ones.

Add a field with a label and a type — text, long text, number or a dropdown with your own options. Mark it required if an idea cannot be assessed without it. Fields can be reordered, and the order applies to the form immediately.

Nothing is lost

Removing a field archives it. Answers already given on existing ideas are kept and still displayed, marked (archived). The field simply stops appearing on the form for new submissions.

The six built-in fields — Idea Name, Category, Context, Pain Points, Expected Benefit and Expected time frame — are shown here for reference but cannot be removed.

Section 06

Manage — User accounts

Everyone with access, and what they can do.

Edit a row in place and save. You can change someone's username, email, display name and role, reset their password, or delete the account. Making someone an admin gives them everything in this guide.

Deleting an account

Deleting removes that person's team memberships, likes and requests along with the account. If they led an idea, that idea is left without a Project Lead — reassign it afterwards. Consider switching them to member instead if they are only changing role.

Most people will never appear here: anyone with a @skedulo.com address can create their own account, confirmed by an emailed code. Use this page for role changes, corrections and departures.

Section 07

Manage — Feedback

Everything sent through the button in the bottom-right corner.

Each entry shows who sent it, when, and which page they were on — so you rarely need to ask what they were looking at. Mark an item Resolved when it is dealt with, or delete it. Resolved items grey out but stay on the list.

You are emailed as soon as feedback arrives, so this page is a record rather than something to poll.

Section 08

Manage — Delete requests

The queue of ideas a Project Lead has asked to have removed.

Each entry shows the idea, its number, who asked, when, and the reason they gave. You have two choices:

  • Delete idea — removes it permanently.
  • Dismiss — clears the request and the banner. The idea stays exactly as it was.
Irreversible

Deleting an idea also removes its team, likes, requests and every uploaded file. There is no undo and no recycle bin. Open the idea and read it before you delete.

A dismissed request leaves the lead with full use of the idea — dismissing does not restrict them in any way.

Section 09

Tasks

A shared to-do list for administrators.

Unrelated to ideas — it is a plain checklist that every admin sees and can edit. Type an item and press Enter or Add. Tick it to complete it; completed items collect under a Done heading with a count. The ✕ removes an item outright.

Use it for the running work of administering the Hub: confirming the tag list, chasing a stalled review, rotating a password.

Section 10

Activity log

Who did what, and when.

Filter by person, by type (Ideas, Accounts, Form fields, Feedback) and by date range. The date filter uses your own timezone, so the boundaries match the timestamps on screen. Clear resets everything.

14-day retention

Entries are deleted automatically after 14 days. There is no export. If you need a record for a review or an audit, copy it out before it ages away.

Section 11

Authority on any idea

An admin sees the Project Lead's controls on every idea, plus two of their own.

Open any idea and you can edit its content, change its status and triage its requests, exactly as its lead can. Two further controls are yours alone.

Editing the team

In the Team & roles sidebar each member's roles become clickable chips. Tick and untick to change them, and ✕ removes the person from the team.

Assigning Initiator / Project Lead to someone new removes it from the current holder automatically — only one lead per idea is allowed, and the app enforces it rather than warning you.

Deleting an idea

A Delete idea button sits in the action row on every idea. It does not need a request first — the request queue exists so leads can ask, not because you need permission.

Use sparingly

Because an admin can edit any idea's content and status, it is easy to change something the lead is in the middle of. Prefer posting a request and letting the lead act. Step in directly when the lead has left, is away, or the idea has gone quiet.

Section 12

Emails admins receive

On top of the emails you get for ideas you are on the team of, or following.

WhenWhat it tells you
A new idea is submittedWho raised it and what it is. Your cue to start the review clock.
Deletion is requestedWhich idea, who asked, and the reason given.
Feedback is sentThe message and the page it came from.
A user account changesAn account created, edited or deleted — including by another admin.
The submit form changesA field added, edited, reordered or archived.

You are never emailed about your own actions, so a busy admin does not fill their own inbox.

Deliverability

Hub email is sent from an address that is not yet on a Skedulo domain, so the first message to each person usually lands in spam. Tell new joiners to mark it Not spam once. Moving to a verified Skedulo sending domain is the permanent fix.

Section 13

A suggested rhythm

Not enforced by the tool — a starting point the team can adjust.

WhenWhat to check
As emails arriveNew submissions. Move an idea to In Review so the 7-day clock is visible to everyone, rather than leaving it at Submitted.
WeeklyDashboard → Needs attention. Clear anything past the 7-day review target, and chase On Hold ideas that have gone quiet.
WeeklyManage → Delete requests and Feedback. Both are short queues; leaving them makes people stop using them.
MonthlyManage → User accounts. Remove leavers, and check nobody holds admin who no longer needs it.
QuarterlySwitch the dashboard to This quarter for a review. Note which ideas launched and what the funnel shows about where work stalls.

Two things to keep an eye on

  • Ideas with no Project Lead. The board shows them as Unassigned. An idea with no owner will not move on its own.
  • The activity log ages out at 14 days. If a decision needs a paper trail beyond that, record it as a request on the idea, where it is kept permanently.
