Section 01

What the Hub is for

One home for the team's AI ideas, so they stop dying in Slack threads.

Before the Hub, an idea was a message. Someone raised it, three people replied, and a week later nobody could say whether it was being worked on or who owned it. The Hub gives every idea three things a Slack message cannot have: a permanent link, a named owner, and a status everyone can see.

You will use it to do four things:

  • Submit an idea in under two minutes.
  • Follow ideas you care about and get emailed when they move.
  • Join an idea's team in a named role and do the work.
  • See the pipeline — what is in review, what is stuck, what has launched.
Beta

The Hub is in beta with the TS team. It is an internal tool on the company network — no customer data should ever be entered into an idea.

Section 02

Getting an account

There is nothing to fill in. Signing in with Google creates your account.

You do not need an invitation, and there is no registration form. Open the Hub, choose Continue with Google, and pick your Skedulo account. The first time you do this your account is created automatically, using the name and email address Google holds for you.

Who can sign in

Only @skedulo.com Google accounts. A personal Gmail account is refused, and you will be told why rather than left guessing.

If someone already created an account for you, signing in with Google finds it by email address — you keep your existing ideas, team roles and permissions. You do not end up with a second, empty account.

Section 03

Signing in

Choose Continue with Google and pick your Skedulo account. That is the whole sign-in — there is no username or password to remember, and nothing to reset.

Because Google handles the sign-in, the Hub never sees or stores a password for you. If your Skedulo Google account is protected by two-factor authentication, that protection carries over to the Hub automatically.

By design

The reset screen gives the same confirmation whether or not an account matches what you typed. That is deliberate — it stops the page being used to discover who has an account.

One session at a time

You can be signed in on one device at a time. Signing in somewhere else ends the earlier session — the first device returns to the sign-in page and says why.

Session timeout

You are signed out after 30 minutes of inactivity. At 25 minutes a prompt appears so you never lose work without warning.

Section 06

Submitting an idea

The whole point of the Hub. It should take you under two minutes.

Press the white + in the top bar from any page. Fields marked * are required.

How to fill it in well

FieldWhat to write
Idea Name *A short, specific sentence. "Auto-draft the PIR from ticket history" beats "PIR improvement".
CategoryOne or more tags. Helps leads group related work on the dashboard.
Context *The situation today. What happens now, who does it, how often.
Pain Points *What is slow, costly or error-prone about that. Numbers help — "about 40 minutes per ticket".
Expected Benefit *What improves, and how you would measure it. This is the field leads weigh most.
Expected time frameYour rough estimate. Not sure yet is an acceptable answer.
AttachmentsWord, Excel, PDF or images, up to 5 MB each. Screenshots of the current process are useful.
What happens next

Your idea is created at Submitted, you become its Project Lead, and it is given a permanent number such as IDEA-015. All administrators are emailed. You can paste the idea's link straight into Slack.

Before you submit

Never put customer names, ticket contents or any customer data into an idea. Describe the pattern, not the case.

Section 07

Inside an idea

One page per idea, split across two tabs.

TabWhat is on it
OverviewWhat the idea is: context, pain points, expected benefit, files, the team, and the comment thread. This is the tab you land on.
TasksThe work. A board of cards in five columns, with the number of cards shown beside the tab name.

The page gets wider when you open Tasks and the team sidebar steps out of the way, because a five-column board needs the room. Switching back brings it straight back.

Reading down the page: the status pill and tags, the title, then a line giving the idea's number, its current Project Lead, when it was submitted and its target time frame. Below that the action buttons, the three content sections, attachments, the requests thread, and the progress timeline. The sidebar always shows who is on the team and in what role.

Note

Led by names the current Project Lead, which an admin can transfer. If somebody holds the Initiator role, a Raised by part appears too. Either is left out while nobody holds that role.

Section 08

Taking part

Five things anyone can do on any idea.

Like

One like per person, click again to remove it. Likes are the cheapest signal of interest and leads use them to gauge appetite.

Follow updates

Press Follow updates and the button becomes ✓ Following. You will be emailed when the idea changes — see section 10. Following does not put you on the team and carries no obligation.

Comment

The comment box sits at the bottom of the Overview tab. Use it for anything you would say out loud: a question, a reaction, a bit of context somebody else is missing. Comments are a conversation — nobody has to action them, and nothing is tracked.

If what you want is for somebody to do something, that is a request, not a comment.

Add a request

Open the Tasks tab and press + Add request. Give it a short name, and as much detail as you like. Your request becomes a card in the first column, and the project lead is notified.

Requests move left to right across five columns:

ColumnWhat it means
Pending approvalRaised, not yet decided on. Everything starts here.
AcceptedThe lead has agreed it should happen.
In progressSomeone is actively working on it.
DoneFinished.
DeclinedThe team decided not to do it. The card stays, so the decision is on the record.
Who can move a card

Moving a card into or out of Pending approval or Declined is a decision, so only the project lead or an admin can do it. If a card is assigned to you, you can move it between Accepted, In progress and Done yourself as the work proceeds.

Opening a card

Click any card and a panel slides in from the right. It holds the full detail, who raised it, who it is assigned to, and its own comment thread — so discussion about one piece of work stays with that piece of work instead of scrolling past in the main thread.

The little clock on each card

Every card shows how long it has been sitting in its current column — 2d, 3w. The clock restarts each time the card moves, so it answers one question: is this actually moving?

It turns amber after a week in the same column and red after two. That is a nudge, not a deadline — there are no due dates to set, and nothing is ever late. A card going red just means it has been sitting still long enough to be worth a conversation.

Join the team

Join the team as… opens a role picker. Choose one or more roles, then Join. The sidebar updates immediately and you start receiving that idea's emails. Leave team steps back off.

RoleWhat you are taking on
InitiatorCredited with raising the idea. One per idea — the option disappears from this list once somebody holds it.
Project LeadAccountable for the idea. Edits content, moves the status, decides which requests are accepted. One per idea, and transferable by an admin.
AI DesignShapes the prompt, model or agent behaviour.
Form / UX DesignDesigns the interface or the intake flow.
Data / OpsData sources, pipelines, integration and rollout.
TesterTrials it and reports back. Needed before an idea reaches Pilot.
ObserverWatching, not working. Observers are not shown as avatars on the board.
One per idea

Initiator and Project Lead each allow only one person. Once somebody holds one, it stops appearing in this list — ask an administrator if it should be transferred to you.

Section 09

If you lead an idea

Extra controls appear once you hold the Project Lead role.

Edit the content

Edit content turns Context, Pain points, Expected benefit, the tags, the target time frame and any custom fields into editable inputs. Save changes commits them; everyone on the team and every follower is emailed a summary of what changed.

Move the status

A status dropdown sits in the action row. Changing it is the single most useful thing a lead does — it is what the board, the dashboard and everyone's inbox are driven by.

StatusMeans
SubmittedRaised, not yet looked at.
In ReviewBeing assessed. The team's target is to clear this within 7 days.
ApprovedWorth doing. Not started.
In ProgressActively being built.
PilotIn trial with real users.
LaunchedLive and in use.
On HoldPaused. Say why in a request so the reason is on record.
DeclinedNot proceeding. Same — record the reason.

Changing who is on the team

People add themselves — you do not approve or assign roles. If someone needs to be removed, or the Project Lead needs to move to another person, ask an administrator. Only they can edit roles on the team sidebar.

Attachments

+ Upload file accepts Word, Excel, PDF and images up to 5 MB each. You can remove your own uploads; the lead and administrators can remove any.

Deleting an idea

Deletion is deliberately two-handed:

  • The Project Lead presses Request deletion and gives a reason. A banner appears on the idea and administrators are emailed.
  • An administrator reviews the request and either deletes the idea or dismisses it. You are not able to delete an idea yourself.
Irreversible

Deleting an idea also removes its team, likes, requests and uploaded files. There is no undo.

Section 10

Emails you will get

You are emailed about ideas you are on the team of, or following.

WhenWho is emailed
Status changesTeam members and followers
A request is postedTeam members and followers
Content is editedTeam members and followers, with a summary of which fields changed
Someone joins the teamTeam members and followers
Check your spam folder once

Hub emails come from a sending address that is not yet on a Skedulo domain, so the first one often lands in spam. Open it, mark it Not spam, and later emails will arrive normally. Doing this once, on your first email, saves you missing updates.

You are never emailed about your own actions. Administrators receive a further set of emails — new submissions, deletion requests, feedback and account changes — listed in the Administrator guide.

Section 11

Your profile

How you appear to everyone else in the Hub.

Open it from your avatar, top right → My profile.

  • Photo — PNG, JPG, GIF or WebP up to 2 MB. It replaces your initials everywhere.
  • Avatar colour — used when you have no photo. Pick one and it stays the same on every screen.
  • Display name — the name others see on ideas and comments.
  • Region — pick one of VN, AU, UK or US.
  • Timezone — helpful on a team spread across time zones.

Your username, email and role are managed by an administrator and cannot be changed here.

Your password

There isn't one. Sign-in goes through your Skedulo Google account, so there is nothing to change here and nothing that can be forgotten. If you need to change the password on the Google account itself, do that in your Google account settings.

Section 13

Getting help

A Send feedback button sits in the bottom-right corner of every page. Use it for anything — a bug, a confusing label, a missing feature. It records which page you were on, so you do not need to explain where you were.

Feedback goes straight to the administrators, who are emailed as soon as you send it. For anything urgent, or if you cannot sign in at all, message the project admin directly.
