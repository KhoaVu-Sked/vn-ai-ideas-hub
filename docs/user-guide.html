Section 01

What the Hub is for

One home for the team's AI ideas, so they stop dying in Slack threads.

Before the Hub, an idea was a message. Someone raised it, three people replied, and a week later nobody could say whether it was being worked on or who owned it. The Hub gives every idea three things a Slack message cannot have: a permanent link, a named owner, and a status everyone can see.

You will use it to do four things:

  • Submit an idea in under two minutes.
  • Follow ideas you care about and get emailed when they move.
  • Join an idea's team in a named role and do the work.
  • See the pipeline — what is in review, what is stuck, what has launched.
Beta

The Hub is in beta with the TS team. It is an internal tool on the company network — no customer data should ever be entered into an idea.

Section 02

Getting an account

There is nothing to fill in. Signing in with Google creates your account.

You do not need an invitation, and there is no registration form. Open the Hub, choose Continue with Google, and pick your Skedulo account. The first time you do this your account is created automatically, using the name and email address Google holds for you.

Who can sign in

Only @skedulo.com Google accounts. A personal Gmail account is refused, and you will be told why rather than left guessing.

If someone already created an account for you, signing in with Google finds it by email address — you keep your existing ideas, team roles and permissions. You do not end up with a second, empty account.

Section 03

Signing in

Choose Continue with Google and pick your Skedulo account. That is the whole sign-in — there is no username or password to remember, and nothing to reset.

Because Google handles the sign-in, the Hub never sees or stores a password for you. If your Skedulo Google account is protected by two-factor authentication, that protection carries over to the Hub automatically.

By design

The reset screen gives the same confirmation whether or not an account matches what you typed. That is deliberate — it stops the page being used to discover who has an account.

One session at a time

You can be signed in on one device at a time. Signing in somewhere else ends the earlier session — the first device returns to the sign-in page and says why.

Session timeout

You are signed out after 30 minutes of inactivity. At 25 minutes a prompt appears so you never lose work without warning.

For administrators, Tasks, Activity and Manage appear in both. Those pages show the Ideas Hub header.

Section 06

Submitting an idea

The whole point of the Hub. It should take you under two minutes.

Press the white + in the top bar from any page. Fields marked * are required.

How to fill it in well

FieldWhat to write
Idea Name *A short, specific sentence. "Auto-draft the PIR from ticket history" beats "PIR improvement".
CategoryOne or more tags. Helps leads group related work on the dashboard.
Context *The situation today. What happens now, who does it, how often.
Pain Points *What is slow, costly or error-prone about that. Numbers help — "about 40 minutes per ticket".
Expected Benefit *What improves, and how you would measure it. This is the field leads weigh most.
Expected time frameYour rough estimate. Not sure yet is an acceptable answer.
AttachmentsWord, Excel, PDF or images, up to 5 MB each. Screenshots of the current process are useful.
What happens next

Your idea is created at Submitted, you become its Initiator, and it is given a permanent number such as IDEA-015. All administrators are emailed. You can paste the idea's link straight into Slack.

Who is in charge

Raising an idea makes you its Initiator — the person who spotted it. Project Lead, the person accountable for driving it, is left open.

Until somebody takes the lead, you have every permission the lead would have: edit the content, move the status, accept or decline requests. You are never waiting on an admin to work on your own idea. The moment someone becomes Project Lead, that authority passes to them.

You can take the lead yourself, or an admin can appoint whoever is actually going to drive it. Nobody else can simply claim it.

Before you submit

Never put customer names, ticket contents or any customer data into an idea. Describe the pattern, not the case.

Section 07

Inside an idea

One page per idea, split across two tabs.

TabWhat is on it
OverviewWhat the idea is: context, pain points, expected benefit, files, the team, and the comment thread. This is the tab you land on.
TasksThe work. A board of cards in five columns, with the number of cards shown beside the tab name.

The page gets wider when you open Tasks and the team sidebar steps out of the way, because a five-column board needs the room. Switching back brings it straight back.

The Documentation box

Under Team & roles there is a box for everything written about the idea somewhere else — a spec, a spreadsheet, a Figma file, a Confluence page.

Press + Add, give it a name, then choose Link or File. A link is pasted in; a file is uploaded. Either way it appears as its name with an Enter button beside it, which opens the link or downloads the file.

Anyone can add. Renaming or removing is limited to whoever added it, the idea's lead, or an admin — so nobody quietly deletes your document, and you can always tidy up your own.

Starred ideas

A ★ next to an idea's title means an administrator has marked it as important. Starred ideas sit at the top of the board, and work on them counts for a little more on the dashboard's contributors list.

Only administrators can add or remove a star. Everyone else simply sees it.

Reading down the page: the status pill and tags, the title, then a line giving the idea's number, its current Project Lead, when it was submitted and its target time frame. Below that the action buttons, the three content sections, the comment thread, and the progress timeline. The sidebar shows who is on the team and in what role, and below it the Documentation box.

Note

Led by names the current Project Lead, which an admin can transfer. If somebody holds the Initiator role, a Raised by part appears too. Either is left out while nobody holds that role.

Section 08

Taking part

Five things anyone can do on any idea.

Like

One like per person, click again to remove it. Likes are the cheapest signal of interest and leads use them to gauge appetite.

Follow updates

Press Follow updates and the button becomes ✓ Following. You will be emailed when the idea changes — see section 10. Following does not put you on the team and carries no obligation.

Comment

The comment box sits at the bottom of the Overview tab. Use it for anything you would say out loud: a question, a reaction, a bit of context somebody else is missing. Comments are a conversation — nobody has to action them, and nothing is tracked.

If what you want is for somebody to do something, that is a request, not a comment.

Removing your own comment asks you to confirm first — a comment cannot be brought back.

Add a request

Open the Tasks tab and press + Add request. Give it a short name, and as much detail as you like. Your request becomes a card in the first column, and the project lead is notified.

Requests move left to right across five columns:

ColumnWhat it means
Pending approvalRaised, not yet decided on. Everything starts here.
AcceptedThe lead has agreed it should happen.
In progressSomeone is actively working on it.
DoneFinished.
DeclinedThe team decided not to do it. The card stays, so the decision is on the record.
Who can move a card

Moving a card into or out of Pending approval or Declined is a decision, so only the project lead or an admin can do it. If a card is assigned to you, you can move it between Accepted, In progress and Done yourself as the work proceeds.

When two people raise the same idea

Duplicates are normal and not a problem — two people noticing the same thing is a signal, not a mistake. Rather than deleting one, the two can be merged.

The idea's lead (or an admin) presses Merge…, picks which idea to keep, and selects the duplicates. Preview shows an idea without leaving the page; Promote switches which one is kept, if you picked the wrong one first.

An admin has to approve it

Pressing Request merge does not merge anything. It asks an administrator, who reviews it in Manage. Merging cannot be undone, so it never happens on one person's click.

When it is approved, each merged idea's write-up — context, pain points, expected benefit, category, time frame and any extra fields — becomes a single comment on the idea that was kept, and its documentation moves across. Its requests, likes, follows and team are removed. Its link still works: it takes you to the idea it became part of.

Opening a card

Click any card and a panel slides in from the right. It holds the full detail, who raised it, who it is assigned to, and its own comment thread — so discussion about one piece of work stays with that piece of work instead of scrolling past in the main thread.

The little clock on each card

Every card shows how long it has been sitting in its current column — 2d, 3w. The clock restarts each time the card moves, so it answers one question: is this actually moving?

It turns amber after a week in the same column and red after two. That is a nudge, not a deadline — there are no due dates to set, and nothing is ever late. A card going red just means it has been sitting still long enough to be worth a conversation.

Join the team

Join the team as… opens a role picker. Choose one or more roles, then Join. The sidebar updates immediately and you start receiving that idea's emails. Leave team steps back off.

RoleWhat you are taking on
InitiatorCredited with raising the idea. One per idea — the option disappears from this list once somebody holds it.
Project LeadAccountable for the idea. Edits content, moves the status, decides which requests are accepted. One per idea. Taken by the Initiator or appointed by an admin; not something anyone can claim.
AI DesignShapes the prompt, model or agent behaviour.
Form / UX DesignDesigns the interface or the intake flow.
Data / OpsData sources, pipelines, integration and rollout.
TesterTrials it and reports back. Needed before an idea reaches Pilot.
ObserverWatching, not working. Observers are not shown as avatars on the board.
One per idea

Initiator and Project Lead each allow only one person. Once somebody holds one, it stops appearing in this list — ask an administrator if it should be transferred to you.

Section 09

If you lead an idea

Extra controls appear once you hold the Project Lead role.

Edit the content

Edit content turns Context, Pain points, Expected benefit, the tags, the target time frame and any custom fields into editable inputs. Save changes commits them; everyone on the team and every follower is emailed a summary of what changed.

Move the status

A status dropdown sits in the action row. Changing it is the single most useful thing a lead does — it is what the board, the dashboard and everyone's inbox are driven by.

StatusMeans
SubmittedRaised, not yet looked at.
In ReviewBeing assessed. The team's target is to clear this within 7 days.
ApprovedWorth doing. Not started.
In ProgressActively being built.
PilotIn trial with real users.
LaunchedLive and in use.
On HoldPaused. Say why in a request so the reason is on record.
DeclinedNot proceeding. Same — record the reason.

Changing who is on the team

People add themselves — you do not approve or assign roles. If someone needs to be removed, or the Project Lead needs to move to another person, ask an administrator. Only they can edit roles on the team sidebar.

Attachments

+ Upload file accepts Word, Excel, PDF and images up to 5 MB each. You can remove your own uploads; the lead and administrators can remove any.

Deleting an idea

Deletion is deliberately two-handed:

  • The Project Lead presses Request deletion and gives a reason. A banner appears on the idea and administrators are emailed.
  • An administrator reviews the request and either deletes the idea or dismisses it. You are not able to delete an idea yourself.
Irreversible

Deleting an idea also removes its team, likes, requests and uploaded files. There is no undo.

Section 10

Emails you will get

You are emailed about ideas you are on the team of, or following.

WhenWho is emailed
Status changesTeam members and followers
A request is postedTeam members and followers
Content is editedTeam members and followers, with a summary of which fields changed
Someone joins the teamTeam members and followers
Check your spam folder once

Hub emails come from a sending address that is not yet on a Skedulo domain, so the first one often lands in spam. Open it, mark it Not spam, and later emails will arrive normally. Doing this once, on your first email, saves you missing updates.

You are never emailed about your own actions. Administrators receive a further set of emails — new submissions, deletion requests, feedback and account changes — listed in the Administrator guide.

Section 11

Your profile

How you appear to everyone else in the Hub.

Open it from your avatar, top right → My profile.

  • Photo — PNG, JPG, GIF or WebP up to 2 MB. It replaces your initials everywhere.
  • Avatar colour — used when you have no photo. Pick one and it stays the same on every screen.
  • Display name — the name others see on ideas and comments.
  • Region — pick one of VN, AU, UK or US.
  • Timezone — helpful on a team spread across time zones.

Your username, email and role are managed by an administrator and cannot be changed here.

Your password

There isn't one. Sign-in goes through your Skedulo Google account, so there is nothing to change here and nothing that can be forgotten. If you need to change the password on the Google account itself, do that in your Google account settings.

Section 12

The Learning Hub

Training tracks you work through at your own pace, ordered by seniority level.

The Learning Hub is a second thing living in the same app as the ideas board. Where the board is about ideas, the Hub is about you: a set of tracks, each holding courses grouped by seniority level, that you enroll in and work through yourself.

Your seniority level is one of five, and an admin sets it for you — you cannot change your own:

PositionWhat your roadmap expects of you
InternThe Intern tier of courses.
JuniorIntern and Junior.
Mid levelIntern through Mid level.
SeniorIntern through Senior.
PrincipalEvery tier — the full roadmap.

The tiers are cumulative, and once you have finished your own tier you get one tier of early access above it — so a Junior who is done with Junior can already see Mid level work.

The dashboard uses different names

Your dashboard’s “Progress by level” chart regroups these five positions into four named levels — Foundations, Applied, Intermediate, Advanced — so a Junior sees a bar labelled Applied. It is the same ladder under a different set of names, and only that one chart uses them. Section 14 has the mapping.

Your level decides which courses your roadmap expects of you, and it is what the locking rules in section 14 are built on. If your level looks wrong, ask an admin — it changes what you see.

Enrolling in a track

Open Learning Hub in the top bar. The page has two lists: Your tracks, the ones you are on, and Suggested tracks, which shows every track — including ones you have already joined, so a track you are on appears in both.

A track card carries a course count, an Enrolled badge if you are on it, and one link: Preview roadmap →. There is no Enroll button on the card. The link opens the roadmap in a panel, grouped by stage, and the Enroll button sits in that panel’s header — so you always see what you are joining before you join it.

There is no approval step: nobody has to let you in, and you can be on more than one track at once. Once joined, the button reads Enrolled ✓ — and clicking it again unenrolls you, so take care not to click it to confirm anything.

The badge on your card becomes Completed once every course in that track is complete for you.

Worth knowing

Completion is per person. Someone else finishing a track has no effect on your badge.

Section 13

Your Journey

Your roadmap: what to take, in what order, and when you plan to finish it.

A strip across the top carries your avatar, your name, a pill per enrolled track, and how far you are through the courses your own level expects — for example 0 of 19 core courses complete, with a bar.

Below it, the Your Journey panel holds a track filter, a Reset button, and the roadmap table itself, ordered Intern through Principal across every track you are on. Its columns are #, Course, Track, Platform, Est. hrs, Target and Status. Target shows a dash until you set one.

Clicking a row expands it to reveal a link out to the course on its own platform, an After this course line describing what you should be able to do, and the Wrap-up button.

Every course on your roadmap sits in one of four states:

StateMeaning
Not startedAvailable, untouched.
In progressThe one you are on. Usually set for you — see Up next below.
CompleteYou finished its wrap-up quiz.
SkippedPassed over via Skip prerequisite on the mind map (section 14). Recorded, not hidden.

Order, dates, and Up next

You can drag rows to reorder them, but only within the same level — a course cannot be dragged into a different tier. Reordering is also switched off while you have a single track filtered; clear the filter back to all tracks to rearrange.

The Up next card shows the next two courses you have not finished, dated ones first. Three controls sit on it:

ControlWhat it does
🪄 Auto ScheduleThe only always-visible action, and a labelled button rather than an icon. See below.
⋯ menuHolds the two less frequent actions: Refresh, which re-checks which courses qualify, and Edit dates.
✓ DoneWhile you are editing dates, this replaces the ⋯ menu in the same spot, so leaving edit mode never means reopening a menu. Nothing is saved until you press it.

Each row shows the course, its status, and either Target with a date or No target set, followed by the estimated hours. When everything is done the card reads “Nothing left to plan — every course is complete or skipped.”

A target date is a plan, not a deadline. Nothing enforces it, nothing chases you, and you can change it whenever you like.

Whichever course is top of Up next flips itself from Not started to In progress automatically. It never overwrites real progress — a course already further along is left alone.

Auto Schedule and Google Calendar

Auto Schedule books actual study time on your Google Calendar. Pick a level range and a Complete by date, and it places one study block per unfinished course in that range.

The Complete by date defaults to the next annual review date, which an admin sets centrally. Quick-pick chips offer that date plus 3 and 6 months, or you can type any date.

BehaviourDetail
Block lengthOne session per course, capped at 4 hours however long the course estimate says.
Existing commitmentsIt reads your calendar's busy times and avoids them, and avoids double-booking its own blocks.
Running it againUpdates the blocks it already made rather than creating duplicates. If you deleted one yourself, it makes a new one.
Target datesThe dates it picks appear on Up next straight away — the same field the pencil writes to.

The first time, you will be asked to connect Google Calendar. This is a separate permission from signing in — connecting it lets the app write study blocks, and it is not part of your login. If the connection later expires or you revoke it, the app notices and asks you to reconnect rather than failing quietly.

The wrap-up quiz

Each course's expanded row carries a Wrap-up button. The quiz is one question per card: click any option and it tells you immediately whether it was right. A wrong answer says so and stays clickable — there is no attempt limit and no lock-out. A right answer reveals the reasoning and unlocks Next question.

Finishing the last question marks the course Complete and records how many you got right on the first click. That first-try figure is what your accuracy percentage means, everywhere it appears.

Not every course has one

Some courses have no quiz written yet. Those say so plainly rather than dead-ending. You can retake any quiz as often as you like; retaking simply overwrites the previous result.

The Knowledge artifacts card lists your three most recently completed courses with their question count, when you finished, and your first-try accuracy. Courses completed before this existed show “No quiz data recorded” rather than an invented number.

Reset

Irreversible

Reset clears your entire roadmap back to Not started — every completion, skip and target date. It also deletes the study blocks Auto Schedule put on your Google Calendar. There is no undo.

Section 14

My Dashboard

Called My Dashboard in the top bar. Where your progress is measured, and where locked courses actually live.

Four tiles head the page:

TileWhat it counts
Roadmap completeOf what is expected of you by now — your tier and below, plus one tier of early access once yours is done — how much is complete. Not the whole track.
LevelYour position, and the next rung up. “Top of the ladder” at Principal.
Weekly streakConsecutive Monday–Sunday weeks in which you completed a course booked through Auto Schedule.
Ideas shippedIdeas you raised on the board that reached Launched.
Why your streak may read 0

The streak only counts courses that were scheduled through Auto Schedule. Finish a dozen courses without ever using it and the streak stays at 0 — that is correct, not broken. Auto Schedule is the only thing in the app that resembles a booked study session, so it is the only signal available.

Progress by level

This chart is the one place the ladder is renamed. Your five positions are regrouped into four display levels, so the name here will not match the position on your profile:

Shown asYour position
FoundationsIntern
AppliedJunior
IntermediateMid level
AdvancedSenior and Principal, sharing one bar

It is display only. Every rule that actually gates a course — locking, ordering, what your roadmap expects — uses the five positions, not these four names.

Below the bars, My courses is the same list as Your Journey, not a read-only copy: you can drag to reorder and open a wrap-up quiz from here exactly as you can there.

The mind map, and courses that are locked

The mind map lives here, at the bottom of this page, with its own track filter. It is the only place a course shows as Locked, and the only place Skip prerequisite appears — not on Your Journey.

A course is Locked until every course in the tier below it is complete or skipped. The gate is per tier, not per course, and the label says which tier is in the way — for example “Requires all Intern courses”. An empty tier blocks nothing.

Skip prerequisite unlocks the whole tier at once, not just the course you clicked, by marking everything in the tier below as skipped. You confirm first, and the wording is deliberate: the skip is recorded on your roadmap and is visible to your manager.

The rest of the page

CardWhat it tells you
Learning activityCourses completed, hours logged, in progress, and your current streak. All-time, not this month.
Confidence by skillYour quiz accuracy grouped by skill rather than by course. A skill you have not started any course toward is left out entirely rather than shown at zero.
What’s nextThe same next pick as Your Journey’s Up next, plus a nudge drawn from the last course you finished.
ApplicationEvery idea you have raised, with its board status. This is the one place the two halves of the app meet.
Not built yet

Two things people reasonably expect are genuinely absent. AI-generated summaries and exams — the mind map is a roadmap view, not generated study material. And reminders: Auto Schedule puts time on your calendar, but nothing nudges you when it arrives. Neither is hiding; neither is there.

The dashboard also cannot tell you which course or skill an idea came from. Nothing in the app links the two, so the Application card shows the idea and its status, and no attribution.

Section 16

Getting help

A Send feedback button sits in the bottom-right corner of every page. Use it for anything — a bug, a confusing label, a missing feature. It records which page you were on, so you do not need to explain where you were.

Feedback goes straight to the administrators, who are emailed as soon as you send it. For anything urgent, or if you cannot sign in at all, message the project admin directly.
